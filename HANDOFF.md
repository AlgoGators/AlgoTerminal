## Honest starting point

The strategy is a mean-reversion and carry position on the refining margin. Its
measured edge is weak and, on every clean basis tested, not statistically
significant. The earlier headline figures were falsified by four defects (a
contract-roll artifact in the price panel, current-vintage storage data in the
H1 gate, an in-sample evaluation window, and an understated trade cost). Those
are fixed, and the defects cannot silently return: the gates below fail loudly.

Nothing here is certifiable yet. The historical window is fully spent.

## The eight gates

Causality, data artifact, trial counter, cost ladder, metric basis,
pre-registration, burn register, plus the invariant lint. Run them before any
claim. Two are human acts and cannot be automated: the negative control, and
writing the hypothesis first.

## The program, in priority order

Each workstream needs a registration before it runs, and a kill rule written
first.

**W0. Start the forward test now.** It needs calendar time, so it is the
rate-limiting step. Log one row per session: date, spot legs, signal state,
position, cost applied, net. Write it to `results/forward_log.jsonl`, which the
burn register already reads. Until it holds 250 sessions with a positive net, no
claim is certifiable. There is nothing to decide here; just start it.

**W1. Carry versus reversion.** The cheapest high-value question, because the
answer changes the whole interpretation. Decompose the P&L into term-structure
carry and margin reversion, using the measured term structure. Hypothesis: part
of the apparent edge is carry, a risk premium that needs always-on exposure,
not alpha. Kill: if carry explains most of the result and is not capturable net
of cost at size, this is a carry trade and should be described and sized as one.

**W2. Model-first margin model.** Build the structural decomposition
(norm plus demand plus supply plus storage plus a reversion-carrying residual)
and derive the signal from model state rather than from binned z-scores on the
level. Hypothesis: the residual predicts better than the naive z. Kill: if it
does not predict out-of-sample forward returns beyond the naive z, drop it.

**W3. Capacity and cost realism.** Model impact against size and find the size
at which the edge dies. Hypothesis: the edge is capacity-limited. Kill: if the
capacity is too small to be worth the operational cost, stop.

**W4. Generalisation.** Test the same machinery on other refining complexes and
on other mean-reverting spreads. Hypothesis: the effect appears elsewhere and is
not perfectly correlated, giving diversification. Kill: if the effects are
correlated or absent, drop.

**W5. Tail overlay, only with real option prices.** Hypothesis: a tail overlay
bounds the gap risk for less than the gap costs. Kill: if the premium exceeds
the risk removed, drop. Blocked until option data exists.

**W6. Execution realism.** One signal function used by both backtest and live,
with an explicit fill and slippage model, so live and backtest cannot diverge by
reimplementation.

## Definition of success

Either of these counts, and both must be documented:

1. A forward-validated edge: 250 forward sessions or more, positive net, hard
   gates passing, at a stated size and cost.
2. A clean kill: the workstream failed its stated kill rule, recorded.

A tuned in-sample improvement is neither. Neither is a longer backtest.

## Do not

- Do not re-tune the entry threshold. It is a disclosed selection, not a
  derivation, and it is frozen.
- Do not treat the historical window as out-of-sample evidence. It is spent.
- Do not store performance metrics in memory. Store mechanisms and decisions,
  and point at the scripts for numbers.
- Do not add a second implementation of any signal or metric.
- Do not put numbers into documents. Reference the generator.
- Do not merge to the public repo's `main` without the gates passing.
